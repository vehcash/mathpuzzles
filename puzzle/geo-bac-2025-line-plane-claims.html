A daily math puzzle for students and teachers

Daily Math Puzzle gives you a new daily math problem to solve: pick the true statements, sketch your ideas on the smart blackboard and check every step of the solution. Each puzzle has eight options, so you have to think about every one of them.

For students

Practise geometry, trigonometry, algebra, matrices, complex numbers, calculus, combinatorics, probability and number theory, from school level to university entrance. Many puzzles are real questions from the Gaokao, the Oxford MAT, JEE Advanced, ITA, IME and the EBAU.

For teachers and professors

Use the puzzles as warm-ups, homework or exam practice, project the blackboard in class and share the formula cheat sheets. Every puzzle has its own link, its source and a worked solution, in English, French, Spanish, Japanese and Chinese — free and without sign-up.

The smart blackboard

  • Draw, then pause. A small menu asks what it is — equation, number, equation + plot, line, circle, triangle, rectangle, polygon, curve, graph, axes, or keep as chalk. Hover an option to preview it; press Enter for the best guess, 1–9 to pick, Esc to keep the chalk.
  • It learns. After a few similar answers it stops asking and does it for you (with a Change button). Choose Always ask / Never ask with ⚙. Right-click or ⇄ on a converted item to change what it is.
  • Type equations in the ƒ(x) bar with live preview and keypad: Enter plots, Shift+Enter places it (click where). Works for y = x, y = sin(x), x² + y² = 25, 2x + 3 = 7, sqrt(6^2+8^2) =.
  • { System: several equations → solved step by step (row operations for linear systems), plotted with intersection points, or placed on the board.
  • Graphs: scroll over axes to zoom, hover a curve to trace coordinates; roots, intercepts and extrema are marked. The Graphs list hides, recolours or removes curves.
  • △ Geometry: points, segments, polygons, rectangles, circles, regular polygons. Select items to read lengths, angles, area; add constraints (length, horizontal, perpendicular, parallel, equal, angle, radius, tangent, on line/circle, midpoint, fixed) and constructions (incircle, circumcircle, altitudes, medians, perpendicular foot). Drag points — the figure keeps its constraints.
  • Move anything: drag an equation, an axes origin or a point directly, or use ✥ Move. Double-click an equation to edit it.
  • Keys: S smart · P chalk · E eraser · M move · V geometry select · Delete removes selected figures · Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Y.
